About me

Different projects, same question.

What's really going on, and what would actually change it? That's the question behind everything I build.

Two things right now. I'm an undergraduate research fellow on Portland State's Computational Modeling REU, where I built an agent-based model of Portland's street network that produces per-segment NO2 emission and noise estimates. Close one arterial in the model and you can see exactly which parallel streets absorb the traffic and the pollution, before the lane closes rather than after.

The other is Once Upon YOUR Child, an AI storytelling app for kids that I designed and built under my own LLC: Flutter on the front, Flask, Celery, and Redis on the back, a parental-consent flow designed around COPPA. One feature explains the rest: I built a limerick mode because I know an eight-year-old who can't read and refuses anything that looks like a baby book. She'll read something she's the hero of.

Research

Close a street, and the pollution moves

Abstract: accepted, DOI pending Code on GitHub ↗ Chapter on PDXScholar ↗ Preregistration ↗

61–76%average drop in estimated NO2 emissions on a closed arterial, across three arterials and 12 random seeds

12/12seeds where estimated NO2 emissions rose on both parallel detours when SE Powell closed

The traffic doesn't disappear when a street closes. It reroutes, and the pollution goes with it. In the model, SE Division and SE Holgate picked up about 32 and 42 percent more estimated NO2 emissions on average. That's the kind of thing a transportation planner wants to know before the lane closes rather than after.

A static land-use model cannot show this: its inputs do not change when a road closes, so it predicts zero change on every segment. The comparison is model against model, because Portland has no dense sensor network to check either one against.
